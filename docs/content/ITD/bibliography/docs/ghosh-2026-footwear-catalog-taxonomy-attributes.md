## Abstract

Abstract The rapid expansion of e-commerce platforms has increased the importance of effective catalog management for ensuring product discoverability, search relevance, and customer satisfaction. In the footwear industry, maintaining a structured product catalog is particularly challenging due to the wide variety of footwear types, complex category hierarchies, diverse product attributes, and large-scale inventory management requirements. This study investigates footwear catalog management through the development of a comprehensive framework focusing on product taxonomy, attribute standardization, and category hierarchy design. The research utilizes a footwear taxonomy dataset consisting of approximately 129 category mappings organized across four hierarchical levels (L1-L4), an attribute repository containing approximately 1000+ attribute records, and a product image dataset representing various men's and women's footwear categories. The study examines taxonomy architecture, attribute governance, visual taxonomy validation, and catalog quality management practices within an e-commerce environment. A structured methodology is proposed to analyze taxonomy consistency, attribute completeness, duplicate attribute detection, and image-based category verification. The research further develops a catalog management framework incorporating product onboarding workflows, taxonomy assignment rules, attribute validation mechanisms, quality control processes, and governance standards. Visual product images are utilized to validate footwear classifications and attribute mappings based on characteristics such as fastening type, toe shape, strap configuration, and footwear structure. The findings indicate that standardized taxonomy structures, controlled attribute repositories, and image- assisted validation significantly improve catalog consistency, product classification accuracy, search relevance, and faceted navigation performance. The proposed framework provides a scalable approach for managing footwear catalogs and demonstrates the importance of structured product data in modern e- commerce systems. The study contributes to the fields of catalog management, product information management, and e-commerce data governance while offering practical insights for organizations seeking to improve catalog quality and operational efficiency.

1.2 Product Information Management (PIM)
Product Information Management (PIM) is a systematic approach used to collect, manage, enrich, and
distribute product information across multiple sales channels. A PIM framework ensures that product data
remains accurate, consistent, and standardized throughout its lifecycle.

name, brand, type, color, gender, unisex, material, size, fastening, net quantity, season, occasion, warranty
information, manufacturing information and product descriptions. By centralizing product information,
organizations can reduce data inconsistencies and improve catalog quality.

footwear categories require different attribute sets. For example, sneakers may require attributes such as
sole material and shoe type, while sandals may require sandal type and strap configuration. Effective
management of such attributes ensures accurate product representation and improved discoverability.
Modern e-commerce platforms utilize PIM systems to support taxonomy management, attribute
standardization, inventory synchronization, and marketplace integration. Furthermore, PIM facilitates
better search engine optimization (SEO), recommendation accuracy, and customer decision-making by
providing structured and comprehensive product information.
The integration of taxonomy frameworks with PIM systems enables organizations to maintain consistent
category hierarchies while ensuring that all mandatory product attributes are accurately populated.

categories such as sneakers, loafers, pumps, boots, sandals, moccasins, sports shoes, etc., each having
distinct characteristics and attribute requirements.

different categories because of naming variations, regional terminology, or manual classification errors.
For example, categories such as "Mocassin" and "Moccasin" may represent the same product type but
exist as separate classifications, leading to catalog fragmentation.

2.2 Catalog Quality Management
Catalog quality management focuses on maintaining the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and
reliability of product information within e-commerce systems. High-quality catalogs contribute
significantly to customer satisfaction, operational efficiency, and conversion rates.
Previous research has identified several dimensions of catalog quality, including data completeness,
attribute accuracy, category consistency, and information timeliness. Poor catalog quality may lead to
incorrect product representation, customer confusion, reduced search relevance, and increased return rates.
Organizations employ various quality control mechanisms such as validation rules, automated audits,
duplicate detection systems, and manual review processes to maintain catalog integrity. Data quality
frameworks typically evaluate product records against predefined standards and identify discrepancies
requiring correction.
Studies have demonstrated that improving catalog quality can directly enhance customer trust and
facilitate better purchasing decisions. As e-commerce platforms continue to scale, catalog quality
management has evolved from a support function to a strategic business requirement.
2.3 Attribute Standardization
Attribute standardization refers to the process of defining consistent attribute names, formats, and values
across a product catalog. Standardized attributes enable accurate product comparison, filtering, analytics,
and recommendation generation.
Literature in Product Information Management highlights attribute standardization as a critical component
of catalog governance. Inconsistent attribute values often arise due to supplier-specific terminology,
manual data entry errors, and regional naming variations. Such inconsistencies reduce data quality and
negatively impact customer experience.
Researchers have proposed attribute dictionaries, controlled vocabularies, and standardized value
repositories as mechanisms for ensuring consistency. Standardization frameworks generally define
mandatory and optional attributes while establishing rules for accepted values.
Within footwear catalogs, common attributes include gender, size, color, material, fastening, sole type,
upper material, lining material, product name, occasion, season, size type, manufacturing info, warranty
information, and footwear style. Standardizing these attributes improves faceted navigation, search
precision, and product discoverability. Furthermore, attribute standardization supports interoperability
between Product Information Management systems and marketplace platforms.

2.4 Product Classification Models
Product classification is the process of assigning products to appropriate categories based on their
characteristics and attributes. Traditional classification approaches relied heavily on manual categorization
performed by catalog specialists. However, the growth of large-scale e-commerce platforms has
necessitated the adoption of automated classification models.
Machine learning and data-driven classification techniques have become increasingly popular in recent
years. Classification models typically utilize structured product attributes, textual descriptions, and
historical catalog data to predict appropriate category assignments. Common approaches include decision
trees, support vector machines, random forests, and deep learning-based classifiers.
Research has demonstrated that automated classification systems can significantly reduce manual effort
while improving categorization consistency. Nevertheless, classification accuracy remains dependent on
the quality of input data and taxonomy design.
Hybrid approaches that combine rule-based systems with machine learning algorithms have shown
promising results in complex product domains such as fashion and footwear. These approaches leverage
both expert-defined taxonomy rules and predictive modeling techniques to improve classification
performance.
In footwear catalogs, classification models are particularly useful for assigning products to categories such
as sneakers, loafers, sandals, boots, sports shoes, etc., while minimizing human error.
2.5 Visual Product Recognition
Visual product recognition refers to the use of image analysis techniques to identify, classify, and validate

significantly expanded the application of visual recognition technologies within e-commerce.

product images and detect distinguishing visual features. These technologies enable automated
identification of product categories, styles, colors, and design elements.
Research suggests that visual recognition can complement traditional text-based classification systems by
providing an additional validation layer. Image-based verification is particularly useful when product
descriptions are incomplete, inaccurate, or inconsistent.
In the footwear domain, visual recognition systems can identify key characteristics such as sole design,
strap configuration, heel structure, closure mechanisms, and overall silhouette. These visual features assist
in differentiating among product types including loafers, pumps, sneakers, gladiators, and boots.
Recent studies have highlighted the growing role of image-based validation in improving catalog quality
and reducing category assignment errors. As image datasets become more readily available, visual
recognition is increasingly integrated into catalog management workflows.

This study adopts a data-driven approach to analyze footwear catalog management in an e-commerce
environment. The research utilizes three primary datasets: a footwear taxonomy dataset, an attribute
repository, and a footwear image dataset. These datasets collectively represent the structural, descriptive,
and visual dimensions of footwear catalog management.

attribute repository contains product attributes and their corresponding standardized values. The image

3.3 Attribute Repository
The attribute repository contains detailed product characteristics used to describe footwear products within
the catalog. Attributes play a critical role in product discovery, faceted navigation, recommendation
systems, and catalog governance.
The repository contains approximately 1000+ attribute records including attribute names and their
associated List of Values (LOV).
